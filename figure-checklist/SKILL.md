# figure-checklist

Check every figure against this list before you publish it, in any medium.

1. Label every number with its unit ("rows", "providers", "DUIDs per provider").
2. When two shares of one group appear, state what each one is out of.
3. Define each term of art in the caption at first use.
4. Make the diagram's structure match the reader's question.
5. Scale axes to readable units (thousands or millions) and keep value labels short.
6. Put value labels above the bars with headroom on the axis. Hide labels on tiny slices and give exact values in the legend.
7. Give each category one clearly different colour, and keep it the same on every chart.
8. Use one series per mermaid bar chart. To compare groups, use small multiples.
9. Never drop or merge data bands for simplicity. Explain why the buckets matter.
10. Let the chart show the numbers and the text tell the story.
11. Make sure the content answers every section subtitle.
12. Cut content that the audience already accepts as known.
13. Look at the rendered output once, with the real fonts loaded, before you publish.

**Why:** Earlier figures had unlabelled units, unexplained differences between percentages, undefined jargon, overlapping labels, colours that were hard to tell apart, a dropped data band, an argument packed into a flowchart, and a subtitle the content never answered.
